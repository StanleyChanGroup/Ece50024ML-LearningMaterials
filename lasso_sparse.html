When does sparsity happen?

With A = I (identity), the soft-threshold solution is:

x̂ = sign(tx) · max(0, |tx| − λ/2)
ŷ = sign(ty) · max(0, |ty| − λ/2)

Each coordinate is zeroed independently when λ/2 exceeds its distance from the origin. Sparsity emerges when the penalty is large enough to overcome one coordinate but not the other. Try setting the target asymmetrically (e.g. x=1.0, y=0.3) and increasing λ — y zeros out first because it is closer to the origin.

The diamond corner on the x-axis gets hit before the y-axis corner because the y-bowl is shallower (target is closer to zero in y). This is the geometric heart of LASSO sparsity.
